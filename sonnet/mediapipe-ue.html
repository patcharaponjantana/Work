Why UDP?
TCP guarantees delivery but pauses on loss — fatal for 60 fps control. UDP drops stale frames silently. One lost landmark packet means one frame of drift, which the EMA smoother hides completely.
Why MediaPipe?
Google's BlazePose runs at 30–60 fps on a laptop CPU with no GPU required, outputs 33 normalized 3-D landmarks, and is free under Apache 2.0. It outperforms OpenPose on speed for real-time games.
Soulslike feel
Soulslike games demand precise, deliberate inputs. Map large, intentional body gestures (raise arm, lean, crouch) rather than subtle finger movements to minimize false triggers and match the game's punishing design philosophy.

Three key axis swaps:
MP z (negative=closer) → UE X (negated)
MP x (0–1, center=0.5) → UE Y (centered & negated)
MP y (0–1, 0=top) → UE Z (centered & negated, so up=positive)
Step-by-step formula
Step Operation Live value →
① Center x  x − 0.5 — basis for UE Y
② Negate & scale  −(x−0.5) × 200 — = UE Y cm
③ Center y  y − 0.5 — basis for UE Z
④ Negate & scale  −(y−0.5) × 200 — = UE Z cm
⑤ Negate depth  −z × 100 — = UE X cm
Why center x/y? MediaPipe outputs 0–1 with 0.5 = center of frame. Subtracting 0.5 makes center = 0 in UE space.
Why negate y? MediaPipe y grows downward (0=top); UE Z grows upward — so we flip the sign.
Why negate z? MediaPipe z is negative when closer (in front of you); UE X is positive forward — negate to match.

Bone Rotations — Position vs Rotation
MediaPipe gives you joint positions. UE bones need rotations. This tab explains why, shows both approaches (FK and IK), and lets you explore the math interactively.
Why you cannot directly set bone X, Y, Z
UE's skeletal mesh uses a bone hierarchy: every bone stores a local rotation relative to its parent bone's frame — not a world position. Moving a bone rotates all its children with it, which is exactly how a real skeleton works.

SetBoneLocationByName only works on the root bone or with physics simulation active — on an animated character it fights the animation pose every frame and snaps back.

The correct approach: take two consecutive landmarks → compute the direction vector → convert to a FRotator → call SetBoneRotationByName.
FK — Forward Kinematics
How it works: take the parent→child landmark vector in UE space, call directionToRotator(), apply as bone rotation. Repeat up the chain.

Pros: No solver, works for any bone, predictable, low CPU cost.

Cons: Errors can accumulate in long chains; doesn't automatically ensure the wrist lands exactly at the MediaPipe wrist position.

Best for: spine, neck, head, pelvis tilt, upper arm direction.
# FK example: upper arm
dir_ue = mpToUE(elbow) − mpToUE(shoulder)
rot = directionToRotator(dir_ue)
SetBoneRotationByName("upperarm_r", rot)
IK — Inverse Kinematics
How it works: feed the UE-space wrist/ankle position as an IK effector target. UE's built-in FABRIK or Two-Bone IK Blueprint node back-computes the shoulder and elbow rotations automatically.

Pros: Accurate end-effector placement; solver handles body proportions.

Cons: Requires IK Rig or AnimGraph setup in UE5; slightly higher cost.

Best for: wrist, ankle, finger tips — anywhere precise world-space placement matters.
# IK example: right hand
wrist_ue = mpToUE(lm[16])
# In AnimGraph Blueprint:
TwoBoneIK.EffectorLocation = wrist_ue

directionToRotator — how it works
Given the parent→child direction vector d (already in UE space):

n = normalize(d)
Pitch = −asin(n.Z) × (180/π)
Yaw = atan2(n.Y, n.X) × (180/π)
Roll = 0 Pitch captures how much the bone points up/down (elevation).
Yaw captures which horizontal direction it points (azimuth).
Roll (spin around the bone axis) cannot be determined from a single direction vector — set it to 0 or keep it from the previous frame.
UE FRotator mapping
Pitch = rotation around Y-axis (nodding). Map to elbow/knee bend angle.

Yaw = rotation around Z-axis (turning). Map to shoulder/hip twist.

Roll = rotation around X-axis (tilting). Rarely needed for body control.

UE Blueprint (pseudocode)
In the BeginPlay event: create a UDPReceiver socket bound to 127.0.0.1:7000.

In the Tick event: read available bytes → parse JSON → extract gesture string and landmarks[] array.

Feed landmarks into the Procedural Animation Component or send gesture strings to the Input Action system as synthetic key presses.

The UE OSC Plugin (built-in, enable in Plugins menu) is an alternative to raw UDP — it gives you named addresses like /soulslike/gesture that bind directly to Blueprint pins.
